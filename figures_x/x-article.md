# We replicated TreeOPSD. The bound did the work.

TreeOPSD minus a clipped TreePO control, three seeds, same hardware: +0.08 exact match on BC+, the long-horizon benchmark where the paper claims its large effect. The 95% interval is [-1.24, +1.41]. On the four multi-hop QA sets the same comparison is +1.98 [+1.03, +2.94] pooled, by seed 3.78, 2.10, and 0.05. Those are the numbers this piece explains.

The paper is *Tree-Aware Posterior Distillation for Long-Horizon Agentic Reinforcement Learning* (arXiv:2610.23181), from Tsinghua, lead author a PhD student. The idea is good on its face. Train a search agent with tree rollouts. Back the final reward up the tree so each turn gets credit. Build a posterior teacher from those backed-up values and distill the student toward it, no learned critic. Reported gains on multi-hop QA are under a point. The large gains, +2.9 to +4.0, sit on the deep-research track.

We ran it on Qwen3-4B-Thinking-2507, the paper's model class, on two DGX Station GB300s. Five arms. GRPO at G=8. TreePO exactly as the paper defines its control, no clip on the update. TreeOPSD. GRPO at G=12 for a token-matched comparison. And one arm the paper does not have: the same TreePO with ordinary ratio clipping. Retrieval frozen. Every arm read at step 100, the checkpoint named in advance. Three seeds for the three tree arms. The preregistration, including what would count as pass and kill, was written before training started.

The added arm is the whole design. TreeOPSD's teacher distribution is bounded by construction. TreePO's update is not. If TreeOPSD beats TreePO, two explanations fit. The posterior teacher carries credit-assignment signal, as claimed. Or any bound on the update is doing the work, and the teacher is one way to spell a bound. Arm E separates those. It has the bound and nothing else.

First, the result against the paper's own control. TreeOPSD passes both preregistered bars. QA average +3.76 [+2.81, +4.74]. BC+ +3.45 [+2.13, +4.78]. If we had run only the paper's arms, the write-up would say replication confirmed, effect larger than reported. The seed table says otherwise. At three seeds the seed-level interval for both gaps includes zero. The largest single contributor is control seed 2, which began to break in its last 10 training steps. Reward dropped from the 0.54 to 0.70 band to 0.22 to 0.40. The length-clip ratio went from 0.00 to 0.27. At eval it made 3.52 tool calls per BC+ question against 1.81 and 1.97 for its sibling seeds. Drop that seed and the gaps fall to +2.40 and +1.51. A pass carried by a control in the act of breaking is a finding about the control.

Second, the mechanism, measured directly. We logged how far TreeOPSD's update target moves from TreePO's on every inner-loop step. Median relative drift at the paper's dose: 1.05% over the full run, 1.56% in the last quarter. Our preregistered inactivity line was 2%. A teacher that changes the update by about 1% is close to the student teaching itself. The preregistration named the follow-up in advance: turn the dose up and watch the gap. If the posterior carries the signal, more drift should buy more accuracy.

At double dose, drift rose (last-quarter 1.89%) and the accuracy gap on our subsets was -0.8. At four times the dose, drift reached 7.74% in the last quarter, and the unclipped control collapsed in training. Reward 0.002 at step 33. 99% of responses hitting the length cap. The resulting checkpoint answers almost nothing. We reran that cell with a second seed. It collapsed again, milder in expression, BC+ subset at 0.0. Ordinary clipping at the same dose trained through: reward 0.49 to 0.64, clip ratio never above 0.10, subset scores in the same band as TreeOPSD at that dose. Unclipped collapses two for two. Anything bounded holds.

Third, the budget. The tree methods match GRPO in generated tokens, 1.03x. In trained tokens they run 1.48x, because a shared prefix is re-scored once per branch during training. Both numbers are in our logs from the first run. GRPO scaled to the trained-token budget removed 44% of the QA gap. Rule for reading efficiency claims: count the tokens the optimizer touches.

Fourth, checkpoint selection. Picking each arm's best checkpoint inside the run, the rule that flatters most training papers, shrinks this gap toward zero. Best-in-window gives +0.3 on QA against +1.5 at the fixed checkpoint. The reason is instructive. Selection rescues the breaking control seed at its step-75 peak, before the damage. The fixed checkpoint was the harsher test for the control, and the fairer one for the claim.

What survives is worth stating plainly. Bounded tree backup is a good way to train a small search agent. The untrained base scores 22.8 exact match on our 500-question QA subset. After 100 steps the bounded arms average 40.6 (clipped TreePO, seed sd 0.80) and 42.6 (TreeOPSD, seed sd 1.07) across the full QA sets. GRPO sits at 39.8. The unclipped control averages 39.0 with a seed sd of 3.95, and that variance is the story in one number. The clipped arm never collapsed, never destabilised, and varied least. If you are training agents at this scale, clip your tree backup. That sentence is supported 2-of-2 collapses deep.

What we cannot support is the credit-assignment mechanism as the cause of the accuracy. At 1% update drift, a flat dose response, zero effect on the long-horizon track against a bounded control, and backup weights that put 85 to 91% of their mass on a single child, the posterior teacher is not carrying measurable turn-level signal in this setting. The residual +2 pooled on QA against the clipped control is a real number with a question-level interval above zero. It declines across seeds and its seed interval includes zero. Candidates are second-order training dynamics. TreeOPSD kept its trained-token count near 43K per prompt while TreePO's fell to 33K, and it learned a longer, search-heavier style, 14.2 tool calls per prompt late in training against 10.6. At three seeds we do not get to choose between that and seed noise. We say so in the note.

Limits, on the record. Three seeds, and every seed-level interval in this study includes zero. One model at 4B. BC+ is 830 questions, so each arm carries roughly ±2 points of eval noise there. GRPO arms ran one seed. Our QA average covers four sets; the paper's average is composed differently, so compare gaps, not levels. Our harness check matched: untrained base 30.8 on frozen HotpotQA-dev against their ReAct 31.61.

The replication note with the preregistration, all five arms, and the seed packages accompanies this piece. Run the control that can break, and the one that cannot. Here the difference between those two controls was the result.
