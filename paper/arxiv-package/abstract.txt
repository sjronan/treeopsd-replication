We preregister and run a replication of TreeOPSD (Tree-Aware Posterior Distillation for Long-Horizon Agentic Reinforcement Learning, arXiv:2610.23181) on Qwen3-4B-Thinking-2507 with search-agent training. Against the paper's own control, unclipped TreePO, TreeOPSD passes both of our preregistered bars at three seeds: pooled exact-match gaps of +3.76 [+2.81, +4.74] on a four-set multi-hop QA average and +3.45 [+2.13, +4.78] on the long-horizon BC+ benchmark (830 questions). Both passes are fragile: seed-level intervals at n=3 include zero, and the largest contributor is one control seed that began to destabilise at the paper's dose. Against an added control, TreePO with ordinary ratio clipping, the BC+ gap is +0.08 [-1.24, +1.41] and the QA gap is +1.98 [+1.03, +2.94] pooled but declining across seeds (3.78, 2.10, 0.05). The posterior term changes the per-update target by a median 1.05% at the paper's dose; a preregistered dose sweep that raises this drift sevenfold produces no growing accuracy gap, and at 4x dose the unclipped control collapses in training in two of two seeds while both bounded variants train through. The budget match claimed for tree sampling holds in generated tokens (1.03x GRPO) but not in trained tokens (1.48x). Best-checkpoint selection shrinks the gap toward zero. We conclude that the supported mechanism in this setting is update boundedness, not posterior credit assignment, and that bounded tree backup itself is an effective and, once clipped, low-variance (0.80 EM seed sd) method for training a 4B search agent.
